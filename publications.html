Through the Looking Glass: Benchmarking Mirror Image Reasoning in Vision-Language Models Accepted

Accepted at CVPR Workshop 2026

Abstract

When a clock reads 2:50 in a mirror, humans instantly infer the true time is 9:10, yet current Vision-Language Models overwhelmingly report “2:50,” hallucinating the canonical orientation rather than reasoning about the reflection. We term this systematic failure reflection blindness and introduce the first benchmark designed to diagnose it: 713 real-world images and 3,529 question-answer pairs spanning 10 scenario categories (licence plates in rear-view mirrors, analog clocks, hands, ears, shoes, chemical formulas, ambulance mirror-text, selfie cameras, water reflections and indoor mirrors) organised into five question types — recognition, correction, spatial, reasoning and comparison. Evaluating 11 VLMs in both single-turn and multi-turn settings, we find that all models achieve only 39–48% accuracy (single-turn) versus 94% for humans, with frontier systems such as GPT-5.2, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Claude 4.5 Sonnet clustered at 42–44%, and performance collapsing most severely on tasks requiring mental un-flipping of text and left–right chirality judgment. Multi-turn dialogue does not improve performance; most models degrade by 1–14 percentage points when questions are posed sequentially.

ML-Based SDN DDoS Detection: Machine Learning–Enabled Intrusion Detection System

CIMA 2026

Abstract

Software-defined networking decouples the control plane from the data plane, fostering centralised control and improved programmability. This architectural flexibility, while enhancing scalability and operational efficiency, exposes new attack surfaces increasingly exploited by adversaries, especially against the centralised SDN controller. Among these threats, DDoS — specifically SYN Flood — presents a major concern, as the controller can be overwhelmed by a surge of requests. The proposed system undertakes a comparative assessment of Random Forest, eXtreme Gradient Boosting, Artificial Neural Networks and Decision Tree classifiers, detecting brute force, DoS, DDoS, probe and user-to-root attacks. It uses features readily available at the SDN controller, avoiding computationally expensive deep packet inspection, and is benchmarked on CICIDS-2017 for its realistic mix of normal and attack flows. Ensemble classifiers — particularly Random Forest — significantly outperform the alternatives on accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score, handling high-dimensional data while resisting overfitting. The resulting framework is lightweight and feasible for integration directly with the SDN control plane.

Credit Card Fraud Detection Using Outlier Analysis and Detection

CONIT 2024 · IEEE · 8 citations

Google Scholar

Abstract

The prevalence of electronic transactions in financial systems has significantly increased the risk of credit card fraud, and traditional detection methods struggle to keep pace with evolving fraudulent techniques. This study explores outlier detection as a robust approach to identifying anomalous patterns indicative of fraud. Leveraging statistical and machine learning algorithms, the research develops a framework for distinguishing genuine from fraudulent transactions by detecting outliers in transactional data. Through analysis of features such as transaction amount, location, time and user behaviour, the proposed method aims to enhance detection accuracy while minimising false positives, evaluated on real-world credit card transaction datasets.

MVEB: Massive Video Embedding Benchmark

Adnan El Assadi, Roman Solomatin, Isaac Chung, Chenghao Xiao, Deep Shah, Manan Dey, Shriya Sudhakar, Zacharie Bugaud, Wissam Siblini, Ayush Sunil Munot, Yashwanth Devavarapu, Rakshitha Ireddi, Michelle Yang, Márton Kardos, Niklas Muennighoff, Kenneth Enevoldsen

arXiv:2606.14958 · 2026

arXivCode & leaderboard

Abstract

We introduce the Massive Video Embedding Benchmark (MVEB), a 23-task benchmark for video embeddings spanning classification, zero-shot classification, clustering, pair classification, retrieval and video-centric question answering. We evaluate 33 models and find that no single model dominates: MLLM-based embeddings lead on classification, clustering, pair classification and QA; multimodal binding leads on retrieval and zero-shot classification; generative MLLMs without contrastive adaptation collapse on cross-modal tasks. Paired video-only vs. audio+video evaluations show that audio's contribution depends on dataset annotation provenance: audio helps when labels were produced from both modalities and hurts when they were produced from visuals alone, a six-point gap consistent across model families. MVEB is derived from MVEB+, a 184-task pool, and is designed to maintain task diversity while reducing evaluation cost. It integrates into the MTEB ecosystem for unified evaluation across text, image, audio and video.

TabuLM: Morphology-Aware Tabular Pre-training for Low-Resource Languages

Ireddi Rakshitha, Devavarapu Yashwanth, Ntakirutimana Pierre

arXiv:2608.26923 · 2026

arXiv

Abstract

We present TabuLM, the first language model pre-trained on Kinyarwanda tabular data. Kinyarwanda is a morphologically rich Bantu language spoken by over 12 million people in Rwanda, yet lacks any dedicated tabular representation learning resource. TabuLM extends KinyaBERT-large, a two-tier morphological transformer, with additive row, column and cell-type embeddings and a learned table-structure attention bias that sharpens same-row and same-column attention. Pre-training uses two new objectives: Masked Cell Recovery, which masks entire cells and forces reconstruction from row and column context, and Column Type Prediction, which predicts column semantic types from observed cell values. We pre-train on 172 Rwandan government tables (~35,000 cells) from NISR, RAB, REB and MoH open-data portals, and introduce TabQA-kin, the first native Kinyarwanda table question-answering benchmark comprising 526 QA pairs across 31 tables and four question types. TabuLM achieves 62.0% exact match on TabQA-kin, outperforming KinyaBERT-large by 5.7 EM points and all multilingual baselines (mBERT 49.3%, XLM-R 50.0%) by 11.7–12.7 points.

KinyaEmbed: Contrastive Sentence Embeddings for Kinyarwanda via Multi-Stage Curriculum Training

Ireddi Rakshitha, Devavarapu Yashwanth, Ntakirutimana Pierre

arXiv:2608.26941 · 2026

arXiv

Abstract

We present KinyaEmbed, the first dedicated sentence embedding model for Kinyarwanda. Existing multilingual embedding models such as LaBSE, mE5-large and OpenAI text-embedding-3-large perform poorly on Kinyarwanda due to severe under-representation in their pre-training corpora. KinyaEmbed is built on KinyaBERT-large and trained via a four-stage curriculum using MultipleNegativesRankingLoss: Stage 1 leverages ~18,000 paraphrase pairs from the Official Gazette of Rwanda with three temperature scales; Stage 2 fine-tunes on 715 NLLB-translated MNLI triplets for entailment structure; Stage 3 aligns representations using English–Kinyarwanda OPUS-100 translation pairs; Stage 4 refines with 2,936 high-quality pairs filtered from KinyaCOMET. We evaluate on SemRel2024-rw and introduce Wiki-RW-STS, a new contamination-free Kinyarwanda STS benchmark of 300 pairs. A seven-checkpoint ensemble achieves Spearman ρ = 0.7298 on SemRel2024-rw, surpassing mE5-large by 20.9% and OpenAI text-embedding-3-large by 41.0%, and achieves the best document clustering silhouette score (0.2146) across all evaluated models.

CRYO-AGENT: Agentic Cryo-ET Reconstruction with Physics-Guided Retrieval-Augmented Diffusion Under review

ECCV 2026

Abstract

Cryo-electron tomography is the gold standard for in situ structural biology, providing a unique window into macromolecular complexes within their native cellular environments. However, the technique is fundamentally limited by low signal-to-noise ratio and the “missing wedge” artifact, which induces anisotropic distortions and structural hallucinations that current feed-forward denoising methods fail to rigorously validate. We propose CRYO-AGENT, an agentic workflow that redefines 3D reconstruction as an autonomous, iterative scientific discovery loop. We orchestrate a Planner–Executor–Critic workflow where the Executor uses Retrieval-Augmented Cyclic Diffusion to inject high-fidelity 3D atomic priors from the Protein Data Bank directly into the reconstruction process. By integrating a Critic agent with a differentiable physics simulator, we demonstrate that CRYO-AGENT significantly reduces missing wedge artifacts and achieves a high Hallucination Rejection Rate compared to state-of-the-art baselines. While the iterative agentic orchestration introduces higher computational latency than linear models, it ensures strict adherence to the underlying physical data.

Adaptive Semi-Supervised Intrusion Detection in Software-Defined Networks: A Hybrid Autoencoder–Gradient Boosting Framework with Transformer-Based Comparative Analysis Under review

Submitted to a Scopus-indexed journal

Abstract

Software-defined networking concentrates critical decision logic inside a centralised controller, creating a high-value attack surface susceptible to both volumetric flooding and control-plane exhaustion attacks. Existing machine-learning-based intrusion detection systems in this domain are predominantly fully supervised, binary-class, and optimised for aggregate accuracy rather than minority-class reliability under realistic class imbalance. This paper proposes an adaptive semi-supervised IDS framework addressing limited labelled data, severe class imbalance and controller-facing deployment constraints simultaneously. The framework introduces a novel Hybrid Autoencoder + Gradient Boosting model, which trains an autoencoder exclusively on dominant benign-traffic patterns to derive reconstruction-error-based anomaly cues, then fuses these cues with tabular flow features and feeds the combined representation to a Gradient Boosting classifier. The approach is benchmarked against ten alternatives spanning classical baselines, standalone deep models and transformer-based hybrid variants under a unified, leakage-safe evaluation protocol. On a 9,123-record SDN flow dataset the proposed model achieves accuracy of 0.991, Macro-F1 of 0.964, rare-class recall of 0.941 and rare-class precision of 0.896, outperforming all comparators in aggregate reliability and minority-class precision.

CryoEMAgent: Autonomous and Adaptive Decision-Making for Cryo-EM Single-Particle Analysis In progress

Manuscript in preparation

Abstract

Cryo-electron microscopy single-particle analysis turns hundreds of thousands of noisy projection images into a 3D atomic-resolution structure, but the pipeline is a long chain of expert decisions — micrograph curation, particle picking, 2D class selection and iterative refinement — that today require constant human supervision. Recent systems automate the pipeline but not the decision process: they rely on fixed thresholds, retry identical recipes on failure, and provide no principled, evaluable model of the choices being made. We present CryoEMAgent, an LLM-driven autonomous operator that runs the complete pipeline in CryoSPARC from a researcher's laptop over a remote MCP-over-SSH bridge, with no human checkpoints. We make five contributions: a formalisation of SPA as a Markov Decision Process; ACPS, a closed-loop adaptive particle-selection algorithm with a provable keep-best safety guarantee; Domain-Grounded Reflection, a cryo-EM-specific failure-recovery method built on a causal failure graph; CryoPipelineQA, the first benchmark for evaluating LLM reasoning on cryo-EM processing decisions; and a visual-grounding audit that tests whether vision-language critics actually see cryo-EM images or merely read the numeric metrics. CryoEMAgent autonomously reconstructs a 3.21 Å near-atomic structure of a G-protein-coupled receptor from the full EMPIAR-10288 dataset (2,756 movies, 477 GB) with zero human intervention.

Astral: Training-Free Multi-Domain Video Anomaly Detection via Agentic SOP Compilation and Symbolic World Models In progress

Manuscript in preparation

Abstract

Most video anomaly detection systems learn statistical notions of normality from large labeled datasets, making them brittle when transferred across domains. We present Astral (Agentic Symbolic Temporal Reasoning and Anomaly Localization), a training-free, three-agent system that detects anomalies by reasoning about procedural rather than statistical normality. Given only a plain-language Standard Operating Procedure and a video clip, Astral compiles a Canonical Normal World Model — a symbolic representation encoding expected entities, states, temporal protocol steps and Linear Temporal Logic safety rules — then detects deviations from it without any dataset-specific training. Agent 1 (SOP Compiler) uses a 3B-parameter VLM to parse the SOP. Agent 2 (Deterministic Perceptual Engine) builds a per-frame Agentic Spatio-Temporal Scene Graph using zero-shot open-vocabulary detection, tracking and CLIP-based state classification, then evaluates structural, motion and temporal divergences via a graph-neural-network divergence analyzer. Agent 3 (Triggered Reasoner) performs VLM-based causal verification only on flagged clips. Astral achieves 100.0% accuracy on LiquidAD (15 videos), 90.9% (F1 = 0.945) on IPAD (66 videos), and 60.0% (F1 = 0.600, precision = 1.000) on a PhysAD 10-video sample, with zero false positives on normal videos across all three datasets.

Scene Representations for Robotics: From Metric Maps to 4D Semantic Gaussian Splatting In progress

Survey · manuscript in preparation

Abstract

Robots that execute autonomous tasks need highly expressive, constantly refined representations of the scenes they operate in. The last decade has seen a paradigm shift where classical metric map representations — occupancy grids, point clouds, truncated signed distance fields — are replaced by neural implicit representations like Neural Radiance Fields, and then by 3D Gaussian Splatting as a unifying explicit-neural hybrid. This survey is an extensive and organised analysis of scene representations in robotics across the entire evolutionary path from primitive geometric primitives to the latest 4D semantic Gaussian field state of the art, grouping more than 130 methods into core robotic modules: dense SLAM, navigation, manipulation, semantic reasoning and physics-informed modelling. Particular attention is given to 4D semantic Gaussian splatting, which combines temporal dynamics, semantic labels and explicit Gaussian primitives to address dynamic scene reconstruction, long-term map maintenance and open-vocabulary comprehension. The survey sets out the most pressing open problems — catastrophic forgetting, scalability, real-time capability and semantic richness — and outlines future research directions.

Can Large Language Models Autonomously Perform Decision Analysis? In progress

Manuscript in preparation

Abstract

Large language models are increasingly proposed as autonomous agents for complex, multi-step analytical tasks. Decision analysis — with its formal influence-diagram representation, unambiguous ground truth and well-established pedagogy — is an unusually clean domain in which to test this claim: the correct answer to every question posed here is known exactly, in closed form, before any model is queried. We study whether four contemporary LLMs can autonomously execute the computations associated with the Oil Wildcatter problem, a canonical influence diagram taught at undergraduate level, together with two diagnostic diagrams isolating specific reasoning primitives. Across 128+ live-model conditions and several hundred API calls we find a graded, three-part answer. First, narrow single-step computations are solved reliably by the strongest models once a previously unrecognised structured-output defect — declaring a numeric answer field before the free-text reasoning field — is corrected. Second, once a computation requires integrating information across more than one step, reliability drops sharply through several independently diagnosed mechanisms. Third, we identify a class of computation — certainty-equivalent inversion under power (CRRA) utility near its asymptotic limit — that is genuinely numerically ill-conditioned, such that no amount of improved prompting can fix it; only removing the arithmetic from the model's responsibility achieved a complete resolution (128/128 correct). We conclude that current LLMs cannot be trusted to autonomously execute decision-analysis computations end to end, and offer a general architectural principle: LLMs should supply only genuinely uncertain judgments, while code performs any numerically sensitive computation.
