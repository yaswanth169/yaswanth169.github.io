Research

My work sits at the intersection of evaluation and autonomy: building the benchmarks that reveal what models cannot do, and building agents that take over decision-making in domains where expertise, not compute, is the bottleneck.

The five areas below describe the threads running through my publications. Several papers appear under more than one heading.

Benchmarking & evaluation of multimodal models

Aggregate leaderboard scores hide the failures that matter. I build diagnostic benchmarks aimed at a specific, nameable deficiency, then characterise how and why models fail on it.

Reflection blindness. In Through the Looking Glass we show that vision-language models systematically hallucinate the canonical orientation of mirrored content rather than reasoning about the reflection. The benchmark covers 713 real-world images and 3,529 QA pairs across 10 scenario categories (licence plates in rear-view mirrors, analog clocks, hands, ears, shoes, chemical formulas, ambulance mirror-text, selfie cameras, water reflections, indoor mirrors) organised into five question types. All 11 evaluated models land at 39–48% against 94% for humans, and performance collapses hardest on mental un-flipping of text and left–right chirality judgment. Multi-turn dialogue degrades most models by 1–14 points.

Video embeddings. MVEB is a 23-task benchmark derived from a 184-task pool, designed to preserve task diversity while cutting evaluation cost. Evaluating 33 models shows no single winner: MLLM-based embeddings lead on classification, clustering, pair classification and QA; multimodal binding leads on retrieval and zero-shot classification; generative MLLMs without contrastive adaptation collapse on cross-modal tasks. A paired video-only versus audio+video evaluation isolates a provenance effect: audio helps when labels were produced from both modalities and hurts when they came from visuals alone.

Reasoning under known ground truth. Can Large Language Models Autonomously Perform Decision Analysis? uses the Oil Wildcatter influence diagram, where every correct answer is known in closed form before any model is queried. Across 128+ live conditions and several hundred API calls we find a graded answer: single-step computations are reliable once a structured-output defect is corrected; multi-step integration degrades sharply through several independently diagnosed mechanisms; and certainty-equivalent inversion under CRRA utility near its asymptotic limit is genuinely ill-conditioned, unfixable by any prompting strategy.

Edge deployment. EdgeMedBench evaluates 45 configurations across five small language models and three quantization levels for medical question answering under edge constraints.

Agentic AI & autonomous systems

Existing systems automate scientific pipelines but not the decision process: they rely on fixed thresholds, retry identical recipes on failure, and offer no evaluable model of the choices being made. My work here formalises those decisions and makes them auditable.

CryoEMAgent formalises single-particle analysis as a Markov Decision Process and contributes an adaptive particle-selection algorithm with a provable keep-best safety guarantee, a domain-grounded reflection method built on a causal failure graph, the first benchmark for LLM reasoning on cryo-EM processing decisions, and a visual-grounding audit testing whether vision-language critics actually see the images or merely read the metrics.

Astral detects video anomalies by reasoning about procedural rather than statistical normality. Given only a plain-language Standard Operating Procedure and a clip, three agents compile a Canonical Normal World Model encoding expected entities, states, protocol steps and Linear Temporal Logic safety rules, then detect deviations with no dataset-specific training. It reaches 100% on LiquidAD, 90.9% (F1 = 0.945) on IPAD, and 60.0% with zero false positives on a PhysAD sample.

TRACE and APISHIFT build verifiable reinforcement learning environments for network troubleshooting agents and API contract migration: settings where an agent's action can be checked against ground truth rather than scored by a model.

Scene Representations for Robotics surveys over 130 methods across dense SLAM, navigation, manipulation, semantic reasoning and physics-informed modelling, tracing the path from occupancy grids and TSDFs through NeRF to 4D semantic Gaussian splatting.

Medical & scientific imaging

Conditional MRI generation: my main ongoing project, an unpaired diffusion model for conditional generation of MRI scans from segmentation maps, in collaboration with Harvard Medical School. Expected duration one year.

CRYO-AGENT addresses the two fundamental limits of cryo-electron tomography: low signal-to-noise ratio and the missing wedge artifact, which induces anisotropic distortions and structural hallucinations that feed-forward denoisers never validate. It reframes 3D reconstruction as an autonomous scientific discovery loop: a Planner–Executor–Critic workflow where the Executor uses Retrieval-Augmented Cyclic Diffusion to inject high-fidelity atomic priors from the Protein Data Bank directly into reconstruction, and a Critic agent backed by a differentiable physics simulator enforces adherence to the physical data.

EdgeMedBench covers medical question answering under the memory and latency budgets of edge hardware.

Low-resource language representation

Kinyarwanda is a morphologically rich Bantu language spoken by over 12 million people in Rwanda, and is severely under-represented in the pre-training corpora behind models such as LaBSE, mE5‑large and OpenAI text‑embedding‑3‑large.

KinyaEmbed is the first dedicated sentence embedding model for the language, built on KinyaBERT‑large and trained through a four-stage curriculum using MultipleNegativesRankingLoss: paraphrase pairs from the Official Gazette of Rwanda, NLLB-translated MNLI triplets for entailment structure, English–Kinyarwanda OPUS‑100 alignment, and a final refinement on pairs filtered from KinyaCOMET. It also introduces Wiki‑RW‑STS, a contamination-free Kinyarwanda STS benchmark.

TabuLM is the first language model pre-trained on Kinyarwanda tabular data, extending KinyaBERT‑large with row, column and cell-type embeddings and a learned table-structure attention bias. Two new objectives, Masked Cell Recovery and Column Type Prediction, are pre-trained on 172 Rwandan government tables from NISR, RAB, REB and MoH open-data portals. It ships with TabQA‑kin, the first native Kinyarwanda table question-answering benchmark.

Network security & intrusion detection

Software-defined networking concentrates decision logic in a centralised controller, creating a high-value attack surface vulnerable to both volumetric flooding and control-plane exhaustion.

ML-Based SDN DDoS Detection compares Random Forest, XGBoost, artificial neural networks and decision trees on CICIDS‑2017, detecting brute force, DoS, DDoS, probe and user-to-root attacks using only features already available at the controller, with no deep packet inspection.

Adaptive Semi-Supervised Intrusion Detection targets the gap left by fully supervised, binary-class IDS work: limited labelled data and severe class imbalance. A hybrid Autoencoder + Gradient Boosting model trains the autoencoder only on benign traffic, then fuses reconstruction-error anomaly cues with tabular flow features. Benchmarked against ten alternatives under a leakage-safe protocol it reaches accuracy 0.991, Macro‑F1 0.964, rare-class recall 0.941 and rare-class precision 0.896.

Credit Card Fraud Detection Using Outlier Analysis and Detection applies statistical and machine learning outlier detection to transaction amount, location, time and user behaviour, aiming to raise detection accuracy while holding down false positives.
