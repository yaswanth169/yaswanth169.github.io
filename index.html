Software engineer at Barclays, researching how multimodal and agentic AI systems are actually evaluated

I build production AI and developer-platform systems at Barclays in Pune, India, and run a parallel research programme on evaluation — benchmarks that surface failure modes current models hide, and agentic pipelines for scientific domains where the hard part is the sequence of expert decisions, not the computation.

Most benchmarks reward models for what they already do well. The work I find interesting goes the other way: construct the evaluation that breaks the model, then explain why it broke. That thread runs through a mirror-reasoning benchmark on which frontier vision-language models score 42–44% against 94% for humans, a 23-task video embedding benchmark where no single model dominates any task family, and a study showing that large language models cannot reliably execute a decision-analysis problem taught to undergraduates.

Alongside that, I work on agentic systems for scientific instruments — cryo-EM and cryo-ET reconstruction pipelines that run end to end without human checkpoints — and on representation learning for Kinyarwanda, a Bantu language spoken by over 12 million people and largely absent from multilingual pre-training corpora.

Research focus

  • Benchmarking & evaluation of multimodal models — diagnostic benchmarks for vision-language models, video embeddings, and small language models under deployment constraints.
  • Agentic AI & autonomous systems — LLM-driven operators for scientific pipelines, verifiable RL environments for software engineering agents, and training-free anomaly detection via symbolic world models.
  • Medical & scientific imaging — diffusion models for conditional MRI generation, cryo-EM and cryo-ET reconstruction, and medical question answering at the edge.
  • Low-resource language representation — sentence embeddings and tabular pre-training for Kinyarwanda.
  • Network security & intrusion detection — machine learning for SDN intrusion detection and financial fraud anomaly detection.

Selected work

Through the Looking Glass CVPR Workshop 2026

When a clock reads 2:50 in a mirror, humans instantly infer 9:10. Vision-language models report “2:50.” We name this reflection blindness and build the first benchmark to diagnose it: 713 real-world images, 3,529 QA pairs, 10 scenario categories. Across 11 models, accuracy sits at 39–48% against 94% for humans — GPT‑5.2, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Claude 4.5 Sonnet all cluster at 42–44%. Multi-turn dialogue makes most models worse.

MVEB: Massive Video Embedding Benchmark arXiv 2026

A 23-task benchmark for video embeddings spanning classification, clustering, retrieval and video-centric QA, distilled from a 184-task pool. Across 33 models no single approach dominates, and audio's contribution turns out to depend on how the labels were annotated — a six-point gap consistent across model families. Integrated into the MTEB ecosystem.

CryoEMAgent In progress

An LLM-driven autonomous operator that runs a complete cryo-EM single-particle analysis pipeline in CryoSPARC over an MCP-over-SSH bridge, with no human checkpoints. It reconstructs a 3.21 Å near-atomic structure of a G-protein-coupled receptor from the full EMPIAR‑10288 dataset — 2,756 movies, 477 GB — with zero human intervention.

KinyaEmbed arXiv 2026

The first dedicated sentence embedding model for Kinyarwanda, trained through a four-stage curriculum. It reaches Spearman ρ = 0.7298 on SemRel2024‑rw, surpassing mE5‑large by 20.9% and OpenAI text‑embedding‑3‑large by 41.0%.
